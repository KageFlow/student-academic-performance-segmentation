# Student Persona Summary

These personas are **templates for interpreting cluster averages**, not fixed labels for individual students. Populate this document with the actual cluster profile table after running the notebook on an appropriate dataset.

## How to interpret clusters
Compare each cluster's mean values for:
- Study hours
- Attendance rate
- Assignments submitted
- Exam score

Use relative patterns across the clusters in the current dataset. Avoid universal thresholds: what counts as “high” or “low” depends on the cohort and context.

## Example persona archetypes (illustrative only)
1. **Needs additional support:** relatively lower study time, attendance, assignment completion, and/or exam averages. Possible response: offer mentoring, study-planning help, and access to learning support.
2. **Consistent engagement:** values near the cohort average across several measures. Possible response: reinforce effective routines and check whether any subject-specific support is needed.
3. **Highly engaged cohort:** relatively higher study time, attendance, assignment completion, and exam averages. Possible response: offer enrichment opportunities while monitoring workload and wellbeing.

Do not assume these exact three groups will appear. The optimal k and actual persona descriptions must be determined from the observed elbow/silhouette evidence and cluster profiles.

## Advisor action principles
- Treat a persona as a starting point for a supportive conversation, not a diagnosis.
- Consider context such as access to resources, caring responsibilities, disability accommodations, course difficulty, and assessment design.
- Never use unsupervised clusters alone to rank students, restrict opportunities, or make high-stakes decisions.
- Validate recommendations with educators and students.
